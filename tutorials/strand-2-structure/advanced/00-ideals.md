# Ideals in Commutative Rings

## Mental

A **subset** $I \subseteq R$ of a commutative ring $R$ is an **ideal** if:

1. $I$ is closed under addition (subgroup of $(R, +)$).
2. $I$ **absorbs** multiplication: $r \cdot a \in I$ for every $r \in R, a \in I$.

The second condition is *much stronger* than "closed under multiplication"
— even one element times anything in $R$ stays inside $I$.

Ideals are to rings what **normal subgroups** are to groups: the kernels
of homomorphisms.

## Examples

- In $\mathbb{Z}$: every ideal is $n\mathbb{Z}$ for some $n \ge 0$ —
  the **principal ideal** generated by $n$.
- In $\mathbb{Z}[x]$: $(x)$, $(2)$, $(2, x)$ are ideals; the last is
  *not* principal.
- In a field $F$: only ideals are $\{0\}$ and $F$ itself.

## Generated ideals

The smallest ideal containing $a_1, \ldots, a_k$:

$$
(a_1, \ldots, a_k) = \{r_1 a_1 + \ldots + r_k a_k : r_i \in R\}.
$$

When $k = 1$: **principal ideal** $(a) = aR$.

## Sum, product, intersection of ideals

- **Sum**: $I + J = \{a + b : a \in I, b \in J\}$ — smallest ideal
  containing both.
- **Product**: $I J = $ ideal generated by $\{ab : a \in I, b \in J\}$.
- **Intersection**: $I \cap J$ — also an ideal.

In $\mathbb{Z}$: $(m) + (n) = (\gcd(m, n))$; $(m)(n) \subseteq (mn)$;
$(m) \cap (n) = (\mathrm{lcm}(m, n))$.

## Worked example: $(2, x)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$

Elements: $\{2 \cdot p(x) + x \cdot q(x) : p, q \in \mathbb{Z}[x]\}$
$= \{$polynomials with even constant term$\}$.

This ideal is **not principal**: no single polynomial $f$ generates it
(any $(f)$ contains arbitrarily-high-degree multiples but not all
even-constant polynomials).

## Symbolic

**Prime ideal**: $P$ is prime if $ab \in P \Rightarrow a \in P$ or
$b \in P$. Generalises "prime number" — $(p)$ in $\mathbb{Z}$ is
prime iff $p$ is prime.

**Maximal ideal**: $M$ is maximal if no proper ideal strictly contains
$M$. In $\mathbb{Z}$: maximal ideals = $(p)$ for $p$ prime.

**Theorem**: $R/M$ is a **field** iff $M$ is maximal. $R/P$ is an
**integral domain** iff $P$ is prime. So:

$$
\text{maximal} \subset \text{prime} \subset \text{ideals}.
$$

In $\mathbb{Z}[x]$: $(2, x)$ is maximal (quotient is $\mathbb{F}_2$);
$(x)$ is prime not maximal (quotient is $\mathbb{Z}$).

## Computational

```python
import sympy as sp

# Demonstrate ideals in Z
print(sp.gcd(6, 8))                  # 2 — generator of (6) + (8)
print(sp.lcm(6, 8))                  # 24 — generator of (6) ∩ (8)

# Ideals in Z[x]
x = sp.symbols("x")

# (2, x) test: is x^2 + 4 in (2, x)?
# Need r, s in Z[x] with 2r + xs = x^2 + 4
# x^2 + 4 = 2*(2) + x*(x)  → r = 2, s = x
poly = x**2 + 4
# Check coefficient sum: even constant term?
const = sp.Poly(poly, x).all_coeffs()[-1]
print(const, const % 2)              # 4, 0 — yes, in (2, x)

# Z[x]/(x) is isomorphic to Z (set x = 0)
# Z[x]/(2, x) is F_2
print(sp.Poly(x**3 + x + 1, x, modulus=2).rem(sp.Poly(x, x, modulus=2)))   # 1 (constant mod 2)
```

## Applied

- **Polynomial-system solving** — Gröbner-basis algorithms compute a
  canonical form for an ideal in a multivariate polynomial ring,
  enabling decision procedures for membership and elimination.
- **Algebraic geometry** — affine varieties $V(I) \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$
  are zero sets of ideals; the ideal/variety correspondence is
  Hilbert's Nullstellensatz.
- **Coding theory** — cyclic codes are exactly ideals in
  $\mathbb{F}_q[x] / (x^n - 1)$.
- **Cryptographic schemes** — Ring-LWE, BGV, BFV homomorphic
  encryption work in polynomial quotient rings $\mathbb{Z}[x] / (\Phi_n(x))$.
